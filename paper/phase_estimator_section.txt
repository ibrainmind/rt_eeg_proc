==============================================================================
PI PHASE ESTIMATOR: PREDICTED R-PEAK vs ONE-BEAT-DELAY ANCHOR
sub-070, ses-01, all runs; ekg_ilc_rt.phase_estimator (PLL + supervisor on)
==============================================================================

Two ways for the canceller to know where the R-peak is:

  one-beat delay  anchor on the DETECTED fiducial t_k. The estimator sets
                  t_k to the argmax of the raw ECG inside the armed window,
                  so this anchor is exact. Its cost is latency.
  strict causal   anchor on the loop's own phase. At sample n the canceller
                  believes the R-peak was phi*RRhat ago, so the predicted
                  R-peak is displaced from the true one by -wrap(phi at R)
                  times RRhat. That is the loop's own perr, in ms.

Analysed: 26 runs, first 600 s each; 24 kept (2 dropped for RR < 500 ms, i.e.
detector doubling rather than genuine tachycardia).

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE ONE-BEAT-DELAY ANCHOR IS EXACT, AND CHEAPER THAN A WHOLE BEAT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  fiducial vs local |ECG| max (+-60 ms)  median offset  0.00 ms
  sub-sample refinement of that max      SD             1.13 ms
  detection latency (declared - R-peak)  median         15.6 ms
                                         p90            19.5 ms
  mean RR (the buffered mode's latency)                 762 ms

  The fiducial lands on the R-peak sample every time, so its error is the
  sampling grid alone, ~1 ms at 256 Hz. Note the detector declares the beat
  only ~16 ms after it: an exact anchor is available almost immediately.
  The full beat of latency in the buffered mode is not needed for the ANCHOR
  -- it is needed to know T_k, and to have the template updated.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE PREDICTED ANCHOR IS UNBIASED BUT JITTERY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                                       PLL phase  re-anchored
                                      (as built)t_k-1 + RRhat

  MAD (ms)                                  47.0         23.2
  SD (ms)                                   57.6         28.2
  median |error| (ms)                       31.7         15.9
  p90 |error| (ms)                          89.9         44.3
  within +-5 ms                               8.2%        17.7%
  within +-10 ms                              16.4%        33.2%
  within +-20 ms                              32.5%        59.8%
  within +-40 ms                              59.9%        87.4%

  Medians over runs. The loop is genuinely locked -- the signed error is a
  few ms, not a standing offset -- but the spread is tens of ms.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. HOW MUCH OF THAT JITTER IS AVOIDABLE?
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  One-step-ahead RR prediction error (SD, ms), median over runs:
    previous RR (random-walk)            28.0
    EWMA a=0.30 (what the loop uses)     32.6
    EWMA a=0.10                          37.2
    constant mean RR                     54.1
    (RR SD itself                        54.1)

  So beat-to-beat RR on this subject is close to a random walk: the best
  simple predictor is 'same as last beat'. Two consequences:

  * Re-anchoring on the last fiducial instead of the free-running phase
    halves the error, 47 -> 23 ms MAD. That
    part of the jitter is self-inflicted and is a tracker fix, not a
    fundamental limit.
  * The remainder, ~28 ms SD, is irreducible for ANY causal
    predictor here: it is the part of the next RR interval that the past
    does not determine.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. READING
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* The detector is not the problem. Its fiducial is the R-peak sample, every
  time, and it declares within ~16 ms.
* The predicted anchor is unbiased but scatters by ~47 ms MAD (p90 ~90 ms).
  That is wide compared with the QRS deflection the template has to line up
  with, which is why strict-causal cancellation loses most around the R spike
  while the buffered mode does not.
* Half of the gap is a design choice, not physics: the strict-causal path
  should anchor on the last detected fiducial plus the RR estimate rather
  than on free-running phase (47 -> 23 ms MAD).
* Past that, ~28 ms SD is irreducible without information beyond
  past beat timing -- respiration, for instance, since RSA is what most of
  this variability is.
* Practical consequence: one beat of latency buys an exact anchor, and the
  buffered mode should be the default. A zero-latency mode is worth building
  only alongside a better RR predictor, and its residual error should be
  budgeted against the width of the QRS feature being cancelled.
