==============================================================================
WHAT DOES PHASE WARPING BUY THE FIGURE-1 CANCELLER?
==============================================================================

gamma = 0  : fixed lag from the R-peak (the shipped ilc_clean path, and
             what Figure 1 of the paper is drawn with)
gamma = 1  : normalized cardiac phase (template stretched to each beat)
gamma = 0.5: square-root warp, halfway between

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. SYNTHETIC, WITH GROUND TRUTH
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SNR improvement over the contaminated input (dB), measured against the true
neural signal, mean of 3 seeds, 180 s each.

The shipped generator (gen_eeg) places a FIXED-SHAPE PQRST at every beat, so
its artifact is strictly time-locked to R and gamma=1 cannot help by
construction. The stretched variant scales each beat's waveform by T_k/Tbar,
where gamma=1 is correct by construction. Reality is somewhere between.

2. REAL DATA (no ground truth)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Variance reduction inside the beat window (dB): how much signal energy the
canceller actually removes there. Coordinate free, so it privileges neither
gamma, and computed on the signal ilc_clean returns.

HELD OUT: the non-causal template is built on half the beats and applied to
the other half. Two traps were live here and both are avoided. Scored in
sample, gamma=0 looks perfect because a template subtracted from the beats
that built it annihilates their average identically. And scoring the residual
by its R-triggered average over ALL beats also reads zero, because the two
half-templates average back to the full one. The one-beat-delay arm needs no
split: it subtracts before it updates.

main lobe = -50..+150 ms, late = +200..+500 ms (the template ends at +500 ms).

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. READING
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* Synthetic, time-locked artifact: warping COSTS -2.66 to -0.39 dB non-causal,
  and the cost grows with RR variability -- warping misaligns an artifact
  that was already aligned.
* Synthetic, phase-stretched artifact: warping BUYS +0.36 to +1.94 dB,
  again growing with RR variability. The sign of D is set entirely by which
  regime the artifact is in, not by the canceller.
* One beat of latency costs about 2.0 dB against the batch average, and that
  cost is essentially independent of gamma: the two choices are separable.

* Real data: the sign is NOT uniform, and it is predictable. Sorting the
  records by where their R-locked energy sits:

                        record  late/lobe   D lobe   D late
         sub-001/ses-01/run-01       0.23    +0.00    -0.01
         sub-070/ses-01/run-20       0.24    -0.19    -0.06
         sub-070/ses-01/run-01       0.44    -0.03    -0.09
         sub-118/ses-01/run-02       3.25    +0.00    +0.59

  The three lobe-dominated records lose a little from warping (D between
  -0.19 and 0.00 dB). The one late-dominated record gains clearly in its late
  window (+0.59 dB non-causal, +0.49 dB one-beat delay). That is the
  phase-stretched regime appearing in real data.

  The mechanism is simple. Stretching the template by T_k/Tbar displaces bin
  o by (o-ra)*(T_k/Tbar - 1) samples. Near the R-peak that displacement is
  ~0, so warping cannot help there and can only blur a sharp, time-locked
  QRS deflection. Late in the cycle the displacement is large, which is where
  a genuinely phase-locked component gets realigned. So the late/lobe energy
  ratio predicts the sign, and it is measurable before choosing gamma.

* Bottom line for Figure 1: it is drawn at gamma=0 on a lobe-dominated
  record, which is the regime where gamma=0 is right. Adding the warp would
  change it by -0.19 dB. Figure 1 is not leaving performance on the table.
